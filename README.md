# Google Resume Studio — Go-to-Market Strategy

A complete Year-1 go-to-market plan for a fictional Gemini-powered resume builder, written in the role of a Google product marketer. Not affiliated with Google — this is a product marketing portfolio project.

**The strategic bet:** the resume-builder market ($9.5B, 2026) is crowded but has no trusted default. Incumbents charge $24–29/month with dark-pattern billing; ChatGPT is free but generic — and 57% of hiring managers say they're less likely to hire candidates who obviously used AI. Google can win on trust, distribution, and an honestly free tier.

**Master narrative:** *"You're qualified. Make sure the robots know it."*

## Key decisions in the plan

- **Positioning: the trusted default.** The market splits into expensive-and-distrusted (Zety), good-but-niche (Teal, Rezi), and free-but-generic (ChatGPT). "Sounds like you, not like AI" directly counters the recruiter backlash against generic AI resumes.
- **Pricing: $9.99/mo** — one-third of the incumbent cluster, with an over-discounted $59.99 annual plan to smooth the churn cliff when users get hired. Ethical billing (2-click cancel, pause-when-hired) is positioned as a feature, not a policy.
- **Distribution as the moat:** entry points in Google job search results, Gemini hand-offs, and the Docs template gallery drive ~60% of Year-1 signups at near-zero marginal cost. Paid media is the tuning dial, not the engine.
- **Message discipline:** never promise "get hired" (unprovable); always promise "get seen."
- **Kill criteria defined in advance:** pause paid spend if conversion <1.5% or D30 retention <15% at month 6.
